How has migration shaped Australia’s population?

Migration has shaped who lives in Australia. In 1905, 20.2% of people were born overseas. By 1945 the share had fallen to 10.3%, then climbed to 20.8% in 1985 and 32% in 2025, so almost one in three Australians today was born somewhere else.

Where do Australia’s migrants come from?

The map below shows the twenty most common countries of birth among the overseas-born. India, China and the United Kingdom are the darkest, but Europe, Oceania, Africa and North America also appear alongside a long list of Asian origins.

Which communities have grown fastest?

Size is only half the story. The heatmap below tracks yearly growth in each country’s Australian-resident population. Nepal and India show long runs of dark orange through the 2000s and 2010s, while England, Scotland and Italy changed little or shrank slightly. Growth stalled across most countries around the pandemic years, when borders closed, before recovering.

Migrants arrive as working-age adults

The age clock shows why the numbers move the way they do. Fewer than 20% of children under 15 were born overseas, but the share jumps among 20–24-year-olds and reaches roughly 40–50% for people in their late twenties to forties. The oldest age groups also have high shares, a legacy of earlier waves of arrivals.

This fits the visa data further down, where most places go to skilled workers and partners.

Pathways, citizenship and settlement

India has the most places in 2025 (48,326), well ahead of China (20,405) and the Philippines (10,972). The treemap shows which doors people came through: skill stream visas account for 131,948 places, more than double the 52,500 in the family stream, where partner visas make up 40,500. Humanitarian places total 22,285. Change of Status (101,022) is shown as supplied and may overlap the visa streams, so it is not a count of unique migrants. Citizenship matters too: Indian citizens lead the regional (13,193) and employer sponsored (12,762) categories, while China’s largest category is the family stream (10,972).

Where people settle follows population size. Sydney and Melbourne hold the largest dot clusters, and New South Wales (91,570) and Victoria (87,770) added the most people through net overseas migration in 2024–25. Per 1,000 residents, however, the Northern Territory (13.7) and Western Australia (13.3) lead, while Tasmania (4.7) is lowest.

Migration flows both ways

Departures complete the picture. Departures of Australian-born people were 57,800 in 2024, down from 71,180 in 2015, with a pandemic dip to 29,100 in 2020. Departures of people born in China peaked at 84,520 in 2019 and fell to 18,260 in 2022, while India-born departures overtook the United Kingdom in 2020 and reached 17,320 in 2024.

Together, the charts show a population built through arrivals, skills and family ties, and reshaped over time by policy, the pandemic and a changing mix of origins.
